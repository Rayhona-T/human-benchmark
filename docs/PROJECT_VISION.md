# HUMAN BENCHMARK — Project Vision

## 1. Project Overview

HUMAN BENCHMARK is an educational, research-inspired platform where people can take part in interactive cognitive experiments, explore the behavioral data produced by their sessions, and learn how researchers study cognition.

The initial experiments are planned around the Stroop Effect and Flanker Effect. The platform is not a clinical diagnostic tool, a validated neuropsychological assessment, an IQ test, or a system for ranking intelligence. Its early results are descriptive observations from a particular task and session, not conclusions about a person's general abilities.

## 2. Problem Statement

Cognitive science can be difficult for a general audience to experience directly. Many online "brain tests" present simple scores without enough context about the task, what was measured, or the limits of interpretation. That can encourage conclusions the test cannot support.

Interactive experiments can offer a more engaging way to encounter cognitive research questions. A transparent briefing, carefully specified task, and explanation of the resulting data can help participants understand what happened in a session. HUMAN BENCHMARK aims to provide that experience; it does not claim to resolve the broader problems of online assessment or science communication.

## 3. Vision

Build an approachable, transparent learning environment where people can explore how cognitive experiments are designed, how behavioral data are summarized, and why conclusions require care. Over time, the platform may connect participation, personal data exploration, and research-methods education without turning individual performance into a judgment of intelligence or health.

## 4. Mission

Make cognitive psychology and neuroscience more accessible by pairing research-inspired interactive experiments with clear scientific context, understandable session results, and privacy-conscious handling of participant data.

## 5. Target Audience

- **Curious general users** who want to experience a cognitive task and understand what its results do and do not show.
- **Psychology and neuroscience students** looking for an interactive companion to learning about experimental methods.
- **Data science and behavioral research beginners** interested in how trial-level observations become descriptive analyses.

Clinical patients and clinical professionals are not the core audience. The platform is not intended to support clinical decisions.

## 6. Core Value Proposition

Unlike an ordinary quiz site that may reduce a task to a score, HUMAN BENCHMARK aims to show the experiment's purpose, conditions, and limitations alongside the participant's session-level behavioral measures. Participants should be able to inspect and export their data rather than receive an unexplained label or comparison against an unsupported norm.

## 7. Product Principles

1. **Scientific honesty:** distinguish established background knowledge, design choices, hypotheses, and observed session data.
2. **Curiosity over competition:** encourage exploration without leaderboards, intelligence rankings, or pressure to outperform others.
3. **Data transparency:** explain what is recorded, why it is needed, how results are derived, and what is not collected.
4. **Privacy by design:** prefer guest participation and data minimization; make retention and deletion behavior understandable.
5. **Accessibility:** support varied ways of navigating and understanding the experience, while being candid when an accommodation could affect a timing-dependent measure.
6. **Educational value:** use briefings and results to explain concepts and methods, not just deliver a score.
7. **Reproducibility:** document experiment versions, conditions, analysis choices, and known limitations.
8. **Avoid overinterpretation:** describe task-specific observations and avoid claims about stable traits, diagnoses, or general cognitive ability.
9. **Responsible use of AI:** AI may assist development or help explain general concepts, but must not generate diagnoses, make unsupported participant-level judgments, or replace verified scientific sources and human review.

## 8. User Experience Philosophy

The experience should feel curious, engaging, scientific without being intimidating, thoughtful, interactive, visually polished, and nonjudgmental. Instructions should prepare participants without overwhelming them. Results should explain the task and measures in plain language, make uncertainty and limitations visible, and avoid treating slower or incorrect responses as personal failure.

## 9. Initial Experiment Catalogue

- **Stroop Effect:** a task family commonly used to explore how responses can differ when dimensions of a stimulus are compatible or in conflict, such as a word's meaning and its display color. A task result reflects performance under the particular instructions, stimuli, and conditions; it does not isolate a single brain ability.
- **Flanker Effect:** a task family used to explore responding to a target when nearby surrounding stimuli are compatible or in conflict with the target response. Results depend on the task design and context and should not be presented as a direct measure of one isolated cognitive capacity.

The exact stimuli, conditions, trial counts, hypotheses, and analysis rules remain to be established through the scientific blueprint.

## 10. Long-Term Vision

The MVP is limited to guest participation in the initial experiments, a clear explanation of each task, session-level results, and data export. It does not require user accounts or cross-session storage.

Possible future directions, each subject to separate design and review, include:

- Additional research-inspired experiments.
- A personal observatory for visualizing and comparing a participant's own sessions.
- Metacognitive reflections, such as comparing a participant's expectations with their observed task performance.
- A research mode for transparent, documented studies, only where suitable protocols, consent, privacy safeguards, and any required oversight are in place.
- Supervised collaboration with educators or researchers.

These are possibilities, not MVP commitments. Future features must not imply clinical validity or population-level scientific conclusions without appropriate evidence and governance.

## 11. Success Criteria

| Area | Practical indicators of success |
|---|---|
| Product | A participant can understand an experiment's purpose, complete it without an account, view an understandable session summary, and export their session data. |
| Technical | The experiment flow behaves as specified; trial records can be checked against the protocol; invalid and incomplete sessions are not silently presented as complete. |
| Educational | Participants can find clear explanations of the task, measures, analysis, and limitations; usability feedback identifies whether those explanations are understandable. |
| Scientific integrity | Protocols, experiment versions, analysis rules, references, and limitations are documented and reviewable; no unsupported diagnostic, intelligence, or validation claims are made. |

These criteria describe useful evidence for project review, not promised outcomes or scientific findings.
