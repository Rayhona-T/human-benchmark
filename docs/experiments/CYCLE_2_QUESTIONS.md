# Cycle 2 — Scientific Protocol and Literature Questions

**Status:** Deferred work list. Cycle 2 has not started. The questions below are intentionally unresolved; Cycle 1 established working defaults and preliminary proposals, not scientifically approved or validated protocols.

## 1. Stroop

- Verify the selected English-language Stroop task and how language familiarity, reading proficiency, and intended participant audience may affect interpretation.
- Review the proposed neutral list (`BOOK`, `CHAIR`, `FLOWER`, `HOUSE`, `STAR`) for frequency/familiarity, length and display width, semantic associations, and possible color associations. In particular, review whether `STAR` strongly suggests yellow/gold and whether `FLOWER` introduces variable color associations.
- Determine whether five neutral words repeated four times each, one per ink color, are appropriate or whether another list is preferable.
- Review the four color words and possible differences in familiarity, visual legibility, and ink/color salience.
- Justify the three-condition within-session design, 12 practice trials, 60 main trials, and 20 trials per condition. Assess fatigue, response errors, and precision limits without treating the MVP counts as powered or validated.
- Verify the interpretation of interference, facilitation, and overall congruency contrasts against the selected neutral condition and analysis approach.
- Review whether median correct RT is appropriate, which spread statistic to report, treatment of error RTs and outliers, and the 10-valid-correct-response display rule.
- Review key mapping effects, whether mapping counterbalance is needed, and participant language/accessibility criteria.

## 2. Flanker

- Review the proposed central-arrow paradigm, five-symbol layout, two flankers on each side, and neutral hyphen symbols.
- Verify the neutral baseline and intended meaning of the proposed condition contrasts.
- Review F/J left-right mapping, motor and layout effects, counterbalancing, and accessibility.
- Justify the proposed practice/main counts, target-direction balance, response deadline, fixation/inter-trial timings, and optional break.
- Review the applicability of the shared RT, accuracy, timeout, invalidity, and minimum-count conventions to this task.

## 3. Shared Methods and Data Quality

- Verify constrained-randomization requirements and choose a reproducible method; determine how seed, generator/version, configuration, and realized order are recorded.
- Define interruption, focus/visibility, invalid-trial, incomplete-session, and session-level data-quality rules, including when a condition contrast should not be shown.
- Validate browser/device timing for the intended implementation; do not infer timing quality from this proposal.
- Review accessibility accommodations and whether results from alternative response modes are comparable.
- Define participant-facing scientific explanations, references, limitations, and the boundaries between educational usability testing and research use.
- Decide what privacy-preserving session metadata are necessary, what participants can export, and how data are cleared.
- Determine whether any pilot is appropriate and what consent, protocol review, and oversight may be required before research use.

## 4. Exit Expectations

Before Cycle 2 is complete, each experiment should have a literature-supported protocol, justified stimulus and trial design, explicit analysis and data-quality rules, verified references, timing limitations, and a reviewed data dictionary. Completion of software or usability testing alone must not be described as scientific validation.
